# Research Notes: The Development of AI

## 1. Historical Background
- 1950: Alan Turing publishes "Computing Machinery and Intelligence," asking "Can machines think?"
- 1956: Dartmouth Conference (McCarthy, Minsky, Rochester, Shannon) coins the term "artificial intelligence."
- Neural networks existed from the 1950s but stagnated for decades; around 2012 data and cheap compute caught up and deep learning won an image-recognition contest by a large margin.
- 2017: The Transformer architecture (attention mechanism) is introduced; it became the foundation of BERT, GPT, and later large language models.

## 2. Current State (Stanford AI Index 2026)
- Capability is accelerating: industry produced over 90% of notable frontier models in 2025.
- Several models meet or exceed human baselines on PhD-level science questions, multimodal reasoning, and competition math.
- SWE-bench Verified (coding) rose from about 60% to near 100% in one year.
- The U.S.-China model performance gap has effectively closed; the U.S. keeps more top-tier models and higher-impact patents.
- Generative AI reached 53% population adoption within three years, faster than the PC or the internet; organizational adoption is 88%; 4 in 5 university students use it.
- "Jagged frontier": models still fail some simple tasks (e.g., reading an analog clock).
- Trust: the U.S. reported the lowest trust in its own government to regulate AI (31%).

## 3. Economy and Labor
- Agentic AI could add an estimated USD 2.6-4.4 trillion in global gains over two to five years.
- Up to 60% of jobs in advanced economies and 40% of global employment could be augmented or automated.
- 51% of organizations expect AI to augment 26-50% of jobs in 2026, so task support is more common than replacement.
- 62% of organizations are experimenting with AI agents; 40% of enterprise applications are expected to include task-specific agents by end of 2026.
- Agentic AI in labor market: USD 3.87B (2025) to USD 5.55B (2026), CAGR 43.5%.

## 4. Regulation and Governance
- South Korea's AI Basic Act took effect on 22 January 2026 (passed 26 December 2024), the first comprehensive AI framework in Asia. It sets requirements for generative AI and high-impact AI and applies extraterritorially.
- EU AI Act: first provisions in force 2 February 2025; phased in through 2027.
